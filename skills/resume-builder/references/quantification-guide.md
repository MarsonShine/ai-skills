# Quantification Guide

Use numbers to clarify supported scope or performance. A range, approximate qualifier, or industry-typical value does not make an unsupported achievement truthful.

## Evidence Required

- Use figures supplied by the user or source records, preserving the period, units, scope, and estimate status.
- Calculate a derived metric only from supplied data with a clear denominator and comparable periods. Keep the calculation available in the working notes; do not put drafting notes in the resume.
- A job title, employer, industry, work photo, or visible equipment does not establish team size, pass rates, savings, or speed improvements.
- When evidence is missing, use supported qualitative responsibilities or deliverables. Ask for a metric only when it materially changes the requested resume; do not block the whole draft to fill every bullet with numbers.
- Unsupported qualitative outcomes such as increased sales or reduced delays also need evidence. Do not merely replace an invented number with an invented improvement.

## Examples

- Source: “Managed cleaning schedules and inspections.” Resume: “Coordinated cleaning schedules and site inspections.” Do not add headcount or inspection pass rates.
- Source: “Supervised approximately 12 staff.” Preserve “approximately 12”; do not turn it into a generic 20–30-person team.
- Source: comparable records show turnaround falling from 10 days to 8 days. A 20% reduction is calculable; do not describe it as 20% higher throughput or imply a different period.

Relevant metric types include team or site coverage, workload, pass/error rates, turnaround, costs, retention, and training completion. These are questions to investigate, not values to supply.

Before delivery, trace every figure and outcome to its evidence. Remove unsupported claims rather than softening them with “about,” “stable at,” or a plausible range.
